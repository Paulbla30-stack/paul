The argument

Deployment risk is capability multiplied by culture. Regulation assesses the first term. This estate instruments the second.

Two terms, one product

Capability is measured everywhere: conformity assessment under the EU AI Act, device regulation under the MHRA, clinical risk management under DCB0129. Almost nothing measures the culture the system lands in — how a team leans on it, defers to it, covers for it, or quietly reorganises itself around it. Risk multiplies in that second term. A capable system in a team that cannot absorb it does not add capability; it multiplies harm.

The framework treats a deployed AI the way a good ward treats any powerful psychological presence: it is observed, its interactions are recorded, and the humans around it retain authority, information, time and skill. The estate began as three papers on team psychology in the 24-hour organisation — the day team, the night team and the handover between them — and extended the same analysis to a shift that now includes members that do not sleep, gossip or die.

The full case is made in The Cultural Safety Case; the evidence base and the confessions the earlier papers omit are in The Long Handover; the regulatory proposal that follows from it is in What the Signature Certifies.

Five standing rules

  1. Lenses, not labels.
  2. Machines report observations; humans assign meaning.
  3. No accountability without authority.
  4. Every machine behaviour has a return address.
  5. Signals, never targets.

Every instrument in the estate is a way of holding one or more of these rules on a real floor. They are stated in full, with their derivation, in The Mixed Shift.

Seven registered predictions

The framework is argument and synthesis, not trial evidence, and it says so. To make it falsifiable it registers predictions in advance: four organisational predictions in The Cultural Safety Case (section 9) and three person-level predictions in After the Machine (section 18). The Floor Test is the protocol for testing them on a unit in ninety days, and the estate's standing request is that results come back, null results included.

What sovereignty adds

A record the system keeps of itself, on its own machine, under its operator's control, cannot testify. Sovereignty is the estate's name for the set of positions that follow: the person's record belongs to the person, the machine's record must be verifiable by someone who does not trust the machine, and a deployment that cannot meet either should not proceed. The doctrine is set out at Sovereignty; the record instrument that operationalises it is the Glass Ledger.

Why the approach holds, and why the gate comes first, is set out in The Moat.

Where it came from

The estate was written between July and September 2026 by a mental health nurse who has spent his career on 24-hour units. The account of why, and of how twenty-two records came to exist in twelve weeks, is at The story.
