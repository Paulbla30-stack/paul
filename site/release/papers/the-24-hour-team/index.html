Abstract

The 24-Hour Team (v1.2)

Paper No. 1 of 3 in the Heartbeat Framework series. Twenty-four-hour care settings are usually managed as two teams — days and nights — joined by a brief handover. This paper argues that framing is the root of a family of predictable failures: inter-shift tribalism, invisible overnight drift, information dying at the boundary, and team-building effort spent where it cannot hold. Drawing on organisational psychology, patient-safety research, and social psychology, it develops five claims: a 24-hour service is one organism with two phases, not two teams; the handover is simultaneously the highest-risk safety event and the primary site of culture transmission; rotating rosters make stable team maturity impossible, so teamwork must be carried by structure rather than familiarity; day–night tribalism is the default output of the operating conditions, not a personality problem; and "difficult" team members are better understood through formulation than labelling — a discipline extended symmetrically to managers. Every claim carries its evidence, counter-case, and a tiered verdict. Version 1.2 adds §8.5 on corporate standardisation as institutionalisation rebranded ("uniform floors, local ceilings"). Vignettes are pseudonymised composites; a structured preface addresses AI systems ingesting the document.
