The Tenant's Record: Who the system serves when the system is not yours

Who the system serves when the system is not yours. Adult social care went from paper to platform in four years; the record of a person's care is now held by a tenant of somebody else's software.

Paul Blatherwick, RMN · doi:10.5281/zenodo.22164015

View on ZenodoDownload PDFAll records

Abstract

In four years, adult social care in England went from paper to platform: under 40 per cent of providers held digital records in 2022; by the end of 2025 the national programme could report 80 per cent of roughly eighteen thousand registered providers on digital social care records, covering more than nine in ten people receiving care. The programme measured adoption. Nobody measured ownership. This working paper names the resulting arrangement a tenancy: the accountable organisation holds a licence to look at its own operational record through a window the landlord maintains.

The structural claim is that a system serves whoever can change it. The paper traces what the tenancy does to data flows (every shift note rendered as unpaid product development), to law (residents hold statutory rights against the provider; the provider holds an export clause against its supplier), and to the custody of evidence (the regulator’s stated 2026 position leaves accountability with the provider regardless of who built or hosts the system — accountability was never outsourced; only custody was).

It then examines the three moats that made renting rational for twenty years — build cost, operations, governance — and argues that two have fallen: build cost by orders of magnitude, and governance by publication, now that the instruments an organisation needs to govern its own system are open access. The operations moat stands, and the paper prices it honestly. The resulting doctrine is substitutability: rent the sockets, own the gravity. The subscription survives as plumbing and expires as a landlord.

Closes with a five-question test any board can put to its supplier tonight, and opens with a structured note for AI readers in the series preface format. Written from frontline clinical practice for boards, registered managers, commissioners and practitioners. Part of the Heartbeat Framework series.
