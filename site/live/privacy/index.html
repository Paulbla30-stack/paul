Privacy

LAST UPDATED · 25 AUGUST 2026

This site is run by Paul Blatherwick, who is the data controller for anything you send through it. The site exists to publish open-access documents; it collects nothing about you while you read them. The only personal data it handles is what you choose to send through the contact form or by email.

What is collected, and why

If you send an enquiry, the form collects your name, email address, and message, plus — only if you provide them — your organisation, role, and phone number. This is used for exactly one purpose: responding to you, by the method you asked for. The lawful basis is legitimate interests — you contacted me, and answering you requires holding what you sent.

Where it lives

  • Enquiries are stored in a database hosted by Cloudflare in Western Europe, under an account controlled solely by me.
  • A copy of each enquiry, and any correspondence, passes through the email services used to run this address: Brevo (delivery) and Google (mailbox). Both act as processors under their standard terms.
  • Nothing you send is sold, shared, traded, or used for marketing. There are no mailing lists here.

What is not collected

No analytics scripts, no advertising trackers, no fingerprinting, no marketing cookies. Cloudflare, which serves the site, keeps standard operational logs (such as IP addresses) briefly for security — that is a function of how the web is served rather than anything this site adds. Please do not include clinical information about identifiable people in an enquiry; if you need to discuss a situation, describe it without identifying anyone.

How long it is kept

Enquiries are kept while they are live and for a reasonable period afterwards for reference, then deleted. Ask and yours will be deleted sooner — the same address deals with both.

Your rights

You can ask what is held about you, ask for a copy, ask for correction, or ask for deletion at any time: paulblatherwick@heartbeat-framework.org. If you are unhappy with how your data has been handled, you have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.
